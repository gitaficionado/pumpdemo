Training & Events · 2027–2028

AI + CS Professional Development Week

A multi-track week of AI and computer science professional learning for Wisconsin educators, proposed for the 2027–2028 academic year in Milwaukee.

About the week

The proposed AI + CS Professional Development Week builds on the experience of the Preparing the Upper Midwest for Principles of Computer Science (PUMP-CS) project at Marquette University. Led by Dr. Dennis Brylow since 2010, PUMP-CS has worked to expand equitable access to high-quality computer science education for K–12 students across Wisconsin.

The week gathers sessions from one-day workshops to full-week tracks in one place, the way PUMP-CS has run its larger CS Ed Week events with up to five parallel tracks. A new AI strand brings artificial intelligence into the program for the first time.

Who it serves

  • Classroom teachers
  • Instructional coaches
  • District leaders
  • Pre-service teachers
  • Higher education faculty

Workshop options

  • CS Fundamentals: Intro & Deeper DiveK–5
  • CS Discoveries & STEM integration6–8
  • Exploring Computer Science9–10
  • CS Principles: Code.org CSP, BJC, Mobile CSP9–12
  • AP Computer Science A10–12
  • AI in the CS classroomK–12

Participant benefits

What participants receive

The same supports PUMP-CS has provided through its grant-funded programs. Amounts and hours for 2027–28 will be posted with applications.

  • Teacher scholarships to cover participation.
  • Training led by Wisconsin teachers. WI CSTA members make up most of the facilitator corps.
  • Curriculum resources for nationally recognized courses, K–12.
  • Mentoring and coaching as teachers put new courses into practice.
  • Virtual Professional Learning Communities that meet through the school year.
  • A statewide network of educators, school leaders, researchers, and industry partners.

Year-round support

The week is the start, not the end

After summer training, PUMP-CS teachers join Virtual Professional Learning Communities that meet throughout the school year. Teachers discuss what is working in their classrooms and review updates to the curricula they learned earlier in the year.

CSTA members from across the region have recognized the model, and many have attended the meetings so they could start the same kind of community in their own areas.

  1. Summer instituteIntensive, facilitator-led training in a course or curriculum.
  2. Classroom launchTeachers begin teaching with curriculum resources and a mentor.
  3. Virtual PLC meetingsRegular online sessions on best practices and curriculum updates.
  4. Academic-year follow-upReturn sessions, coaching, and peer networking to sustain the course.
